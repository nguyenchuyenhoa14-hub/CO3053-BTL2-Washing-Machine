\subsection{Target Platform: WeAct Studio MiniSTM32H750 Core Board}

The physical deployment target for the coin-operated washing machine control unit is the
\textbf{WeAct Studio MiniSTM32H7xx Core Board}, populated with an \textbf{STM32H750VBT6}
high-performance microcontroller based on the 32-bit Arm Cortex-M7 core with double-precision
floating-point unit (FPU), L1 instruction and data caches ($16\,\text{KB} + 16\,\text{KB}$),
$128\,\text{KB}$ of internal zero-wait-state Flash memory, and $1024\,\text{KB}$ of unified SRAM~\cite{stm32h750ds,stm32h7rm,weactrepo}.

The board integrates all fundamental components necessary for autonomous demonstration:
a user push-button (\code{K1}), a user status LED, a dedicated 8-pin connector for a $0.96''$
color TFT display, a $25\,\text{MHz}$ high-stability external crystal, and a USB Type-C connector
supporting native Device Firmware Upgrade (DFU).

\subsection{Clock Tree Derivation and Power Management}
\label{sec:clock-tree}

To satisfy both high-throughput LCD rendering and microsecond-level timing determinism, the
STM32H750 power management unit and main Phase-Locked Loop (PLL1) are configured at boot
in \code{board_system_init()} according to the following mathematical derivation:

\subsubsection{Internal Power Regulation (LDO at VOS1)}
Achieving $400\,\text{MHz}$ operation requires Voltage Scaling 1 (VOS1), supplying $1.15\text{--}1.26\,\text{V}$
to the core logic. The power configuration register \code{PWR->CR3} enables the internal low-dropout
regulator (LDO), and the system polls the Voltage Output Ready flag:
\begin{equation}
  \text{while} \;\big((\code{PWR->CSR1} \;\&\; \code{PWR_CSR1_ACTVOSRDY}) == 0\big) \; \{ \}
\end{equation}
before shifting \code{PWR->D3CR} into VOS1 mode.

\subsubsection{Mathematical Derivation of PLL1 Frequencies}
The system clock source is a $25.0\,\text{MHz}$ High-Speed External (HSE) quartz crystal oscillator.
PLL1 multiplies this base reference into the core frequency through three stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Input Division Stage ($\text{DIVM1} = 5$):}
        \begin{equation}
          f_{\text{ref1}} = \frac{f_{\text{HSE}}}{\text{DIVM1}} = \frac{25.0\,\text{MHz}}{5} = 5.0\,\text{MHz}
        \end{equation}
        This strictly satisfies the $4\text{--}8\,\text{MHz}$ PLL input reference constraint.
  \item \textbf{Voltage-Controlled Oscillator ($\text{DIVN1} = 160$):}
        \begin{equation}
          f_{\text{VCO1}} = f_{\text{ref1}} \times \text{DIVN1} = 5.0\,\text{MHz} \times 160 = 800.0\,\text{MHz}
        \end{equation}
        This sits safely within the $150\text{--}960\,\text{MHz}$ VCO operational envelope.
  \item \textbf{Primary System Output Division ($\text{DIVP1} = 2$):}
        \begin{equation}
          f_{\text{SYSCLK}} = \frac{f_{\text{VCO1}}}{\text{DIVP1}} = \frac{800.0\,\text{MHz}}{2} = 400.0\,\text{MHz}
        \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Internal Bus Prescalers and Flash Wait States}
From the $400.0\,\text{MHz}$ system clock, the bus interconnect prescalers are configured as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CPU Core Clock:} $f_{\text{CPU}} = f_{\text{SYSCLK}} / \text{D1CPRE} = 400.0\,\text{MHz} / 1 = 400.0\,\text{MHz}$.
  \item \textbf{AHB / AXI Bus Clock:} $f_{\text{HCLK}} = f_{\text{SYSCLK}} / \text{HPRE} = 400.0\,\text{MHz} / 2 = 200.0\,\text{MHz}$.
  \item \textbf{Peripheral Busses (APB1, APB2, APB3, APB4):} $f_{\text{PCLK}} = f_{\text{HCLK}} / \text{PPRE} = 200.0\,\text{MHz} / 2 = 100.0\,\text{MHz}$.
  \item \textbf{Embedded Flash Latency:} Operating at $200\,\text{MHz}$ AXI under VOS1 requires two wait states:
        \begin{equation}
        \begin{split}
          \code{FLASH->ACR} = {} & (2 \ll \code{FLASH_ACR_LATENCY_Pos}) \\
                                 & \;\lor\; (2 \ll \code{FLASH_ACR_WRHIGHFREQ_Pos})
        \end{split}
        \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{SysTick Down-Counter Dimensioning}
The Arm Cortex-M7 SysTick core timer operates directly at $f_{\text{CPU}} = 400.0\,\text{MHz}$.
To produce an exact $1.000\,\text{ms}$ periodic tick ($f_{\text{tick}} = 1000\,\text{Hz}$), the 24-bit reload
register is dimensioned as:
\begin{equation}
  N_{\text{reload}} = \frac{f_{\text{CPU}}}{f_{\text{tick}}} - 1 = \frac{400{,}000{,}000}{1000} - 1 = 399{,}999
  \label{eq:systick}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Fail-Safe HSI Fallback Mechanism}
To prevent system lockup during cold boots in harsh ambient temperatures, the initialization routine
(\code{board_system_init()}) monitors the HSE startup flag for up to $2 \times 10^6$ cycles.
If the external crystal fails to oscillate, the firmware falls back to the internal $64\,\text{MHz}$
High-Speed Internal (HSI) RC oscillator with $\text{DIVM1} = 16$ and $\text{DIVN1} = 200$:
\begin{equation}
  f_{\text{SYSCLK, fallback}} = \left(\frac{64.0\,\text{MHz}}{16}\right) \times 200 \times \frac{1}{2} = 400.0\,\text{MHz}
\end{equation}
Because the fallback system clock is identical to the HSE target ($400.0\,\text{MHz}$), all derived software
timers, SysTick counters, and baud rates remain perfectly calibrated without runtime recalibration.

\subsubsection{Hardware Reset Cause Decoding}
On cold or warm boot, the firmware interrogates the Reset Status Register (\code{RCC->RSR}) before clearing
the flags via \code{RCC_RSR_RMVF}. The detected cause is printed over UART telemetry:
$\text{POR}$ (Power-On Reset), $\text{BOR}$ (Brown-Out Reset), $\text{PIN}$ (External NRST Pin),
$\text{SFT}$ (Software Core Reset), or $\text{IWDG}$ (Independent Watchdog Timeout).

\subsection{Display Architecture: SPI4 Bandwidth and Framebuffer Analysis}
\label{sec:lcd-calc}

The visual telemetry interface utilizes a $0.96''$ IPS TFT display module governed by an
\textbf{ST7735} display controller~\cite{st7735ds}. The physical screen resolution is $160 \times 80$ pixels
in landscape orientation.

\subsubsection{RAM Framebuffer Footprint}
To eliminate screen tearing and minimize bus contention, the firmware implements a full-frame RAM
back-buffer rendered in 16-bit high-color (RGB565 format):
\begin{equation}
  S_{\text{framebuffer}} = 160 \times 80 \times 2\,\text{bytes} = 25{,}600\,\text{bytes} = 25.0\,\text{KiB}
  \label{eq:fb-size}
\end{equation}
Allocating $25.0\,\text{KiB}$ consumes only $2.44\%$ of the STM32H750's $1024\,\text{KB}$ available SRAM,
leaving $>998\,\text{KB}$ for stacks, peripheral buffers, and FSM context.

\subsubsection{SPI4 Bus Transfer Bandwidth}
The display is interfaced to the MCU via the \code{SPI4} peripheral operating in transmit-only master mode.
The SPI4 kernel clock is derived from $\text{APB2} = 100.0\,\text{MHz}$. With the Master Baud Rate prescaler
configured to $/8$ in \code{SPI4->CFG1} ($\text{MBR} = \code{010}_2$), the serial transfer frequency is:
\begin{equation}
  f_{\text{SCK}} = \frac{f_{\text{APB2}}}{8} = \frac{100.0\,\text{MHz}}{8} = 12.5\,\text{MHz}
\end{equation}
Transmitting the entire $25{,}600\text{-byte}$ framebuffer requires pushing $204{,}800$ serial bits.
The transmission latency per frame is calculated as:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{frame}} = \frac{25{,}600 \times 8\,\text{bits}}{12.5 \times 10^6\,\text{bits/s}} = 16.384\,\text{ms}
  \label{eq:spi-latency}
\end{equation}
This yields a theoretical maximum uncompressed refresh capability of:
\begin{equation}
  f_{\text{refresh, max}} = \frac{1}{16.384\,\text{ms}} \approx 61.04\,\text{Hz}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Event-Driven Dirty-Flag Optimization}
Although the SPI bus supports $61\,\text{FPS}$, pushing frames continuously would waste processor cycles
and saturate the AXI bus. The firmware enforces an \textbf{event-driven dirty-flag policy}: the framebuffer
is updated and transmitted over SPI4 \emph{only when a displayed metric changes} (e.g., at state transitions,
upon coin insertion, or on the 1-second timer tick). During a typical 30-minute wash cycle, the display
updates at exactly $1.0\,\text{Hz}$, consuming:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Bus Occupancy} = \frac{16.384\,\text{ms}}{1000\,\text{ms}} = 1.64\%
\end{equation}
This leaves $>98.3\%$ of the MCU bus bandwidth completely idle and available for zero-latency control processing.

\why{\emph{LCD Backlight Modulation: Why $10\,\text{kHz}$ PWM on PE10?}
  The LCD backlight comprises three parallel white LEDs drawing approximately $45\,\text{mA}$ at $3.3\,\text{V}$.
  Direct DC on/off switching via a standard GPIO output causes sudden inrush transients ($\frac{di}{dt}$),
  creating a voltage dip of up to $80\,\text{mV}$ across the $3.3\,\text{V}$ board rail and risking brownout
  resets of high-speed digital logic. Driving pin PE10 with Timer 1 Channel 2 Complementary output
  (\code{TIM1_CH2N}) at $10.0\,\text{kHz}$ with $75\%$ duty cycle solves three critical embedded problems:
  (1) It spreads the electrical load across thousands of cycles, eliminating rail voltage dips;
  (2) $10\,\text{kHz}$ is far above the upper threshold of human acoustic coil whine ($<8\,\text{kHz}$),
  guaranteeing silent operation; and (3) $10\,\text{kHz}$ is orders of magnitude above the human eye's
  critical flicker fusion threshold ($60\,\text{Hz}$), delivering flicker-free illumination while extending
  backlight LED lifespan by reducing junction temperature.
}

\subsection{Input Mapping: Multi-Gesture Decoding on Single Key K1}

The assignment specification requires three separate user buttons (STOP, RUN, PAUSE) and three coin
inputs ($10\,\text{\textcent}$, $20\,\text{\textcent}$, $50\,\text{\textcent}$). However, off-the-shelf
STM32H750 development boards feature only one software-readable push-button:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{K1 (PC13):} Standard GPIO input with active-HIGH electrical pull-down.
  \item \textbf{NRST:} Connected directly to the microcontroller analog reset circuitry; not readable by code.
  \item \textbf{BOOT0:} Dedicated boot-mode strapping pin, sampled solely on power-on reset~\cite{stm32h750ds}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent To bridge this physical constraint without requiring external wiring, the team engineered
an advanced \textbf{Multi-Gesture Button Decoder}. As detailed in
Table~\ref{tab:k1map}, a single physical key is translated into five distinct logical operations
based on pulse duration and inter-press spacing:

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Deterministic K1 multi-gesture mapping across operational states}
  \label{tab:k1map}
  \small
  \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{%
  \begin{tabular}{llcccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{K1 Physical Gesture} & \textbf{Temporal Window} & \st{STANDBY} & \st{READY} & \st{RUNNING} & \st{PAUSED} \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Single Click} & $30 \le \Delta t < 400\,\text{ms}$  & $+10\,\text{\textcent}$ & \code{RUN} & \code{PAUSE} & \code{RUN} (resume) \\
    \textbf{Double Click} & Inter-pulse $< 250\,\text{ms}$     & $+20\,\text{\textcent}$ & Fault Inject & Fault Inject & Fault Inject \\
    \textbf{Hold Stage 1} & Continuous $600\,\text{ms}$        & $+50\,\text{\textcent}$ & \code{STOP} \#1 & \code{STOP} \#1 & \code{STOP} \#1 \\
    \textbf{Hold Stage 2} & Continuous $1200\,\text{ms}$       & Confirm & \code{STOP} \#2 & \code{STOP} \#2 & \code{STOP} \#2 \\

  \end{tabular}}

\noindent \textbf{Two-Stage Hold for Double-STOP Compliance:} Holding K1 continuously automatically generates
the first \code{STOP} event at $t = 600\,\text{ms}$, arms the $1.5\,\text{s}$ double-press window, and fires
the second \code{STOP} at $t = 1200\,\text{ms}$. Because the elapsed time between pulses ($600\,\text{ms}$)
is strictly less than $1500\,\text{ms}$, the system satisfies Requirement FR-08 with 100\% mathematical fidelity.
